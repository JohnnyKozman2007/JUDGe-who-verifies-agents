\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Agreement is not verification. Across 64{,}800 verifications, judges without an environment
signal fail together rather than independently, a stronger generator's errors are
correspondingly harder for any of them to catch, and no verification strategy closes the
gap, so larger panels and better prompting do not deliver what they promise. Blinding judges
to authorship misses as well: models are nearly indifferent to the \emph{told} frame, varying
by under one percentage point, yet approve their own errors 11.9 points more often when they
truly wrote them. That gap survives a placebo-controlled style transfer and dissolves under a
consistency control, so what persists is the conclusion a model still holds, not recognition
of its own prose.

Reliable verification therefore requires the environment. It does not require blind trust in
the judge reading it: verifiers that override a passing test suite are unjustified in 77.2\%
of cases and confirmed in only 7.3\%. The two findings point the same way. A judge left alone
with its own reasoning defends it, and a judge handed a signal it is free to ignore will
sometimes ignore it. What raises reliability is not a better reader but a checkable
environment, and a judge aligned to defer to it.
