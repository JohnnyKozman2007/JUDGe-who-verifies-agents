\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Agreement is not verification. Across $64{,}800$ evaluations, ungrounded judges share correlated blind spots, miss errors from stronger generators, and exhibit an 11.9\,pp self-preference gap driven by belief persistence rather than prompt framing or recognition. Model-side mitigations—blinding, capability scaling, majority voting, and multi-agent debate—cannot overcome flawed generative priors, creating an illusion of reliability through mutual agreement. In contrast, environmental execution grounding uniquely restores verifier integrity, decorrelating evaluator errors and achieving a 93.3\% error catch rate.

These findings offer an urgent design principle for autonomous agent architectures: as LLM-as-judge frameworks proliferate across code generation and multi-step reasoning agents, deploying ungrounded models to oversee other models creates a false sense of security. Reliable verification cannot be engineered through more persuasive readers or complex debate protocols; it fundamentally requires grounding evaluators in external environment feedback.
